## 6. Important Claims That Need Careful Handling

The source documents contain promotional or comparative statements, including claims that Raj Resort is the “No. 1” venue, the largest wedding resort in Patna, the only resort with Ganga River-view dining, the first venue to offer certain entry effects, or the only venue using particular lighting technology.

These statements are **not independently verified by the documents themselves**. Do not present them as objective facts by default.

Preferred approach:
- Lead with specific facilities and factual descriptions.
- Use a comparative or “first/only/best” claim only if the website owner confirms it and can support it.
- If evidence is not available, rewrite the line without the superlative.

For example:
- Instead of “Bihar’s No. 1 wedding resort”, say “A wedding and event venue in Patna”.
- Instead of “the only resort in Patna with Ganga dining”, say “Enjoy dining with a view of the Ganga River” if the view is accurate.
- Instead of “100% safe”, describe the effect as a cold-spark effect only if confirmed, and do not make absolute safety guarantees.
- Instead of “world-class catering”, describe the trained catering team, food options, and presentation.

Do not claim that cold-spark effects are “100% safe”, smokeless, or suitable for all indoor and outdoor settings without verified technical and operational confirmation. Do not call any feature “first in Bihar” or “first in Patna” without supporting evidence.

The source says “400+ car parking space” and “400+ car parking facility”. Use “400+ parking spaces” only if the figure refers to spaces for cars and is confirmed by the owner. Otherwise flag it for confirmation.

The documents mention both “25+ rooms and suites” and “luxurious rooms”. Keep “25+ rooms and suites” only if this remains accurate.

The documents mention “400+ successful weddings”. Treat this as a business-provided figure, not an independently verified statistic. It may be used if the resort confirms it is current.
